# Methodology audit: evidence, requirements, and pre-registered choices

**Decision date:** 2026-10-04  
**Scope:** the approved event-to-stress methodology in `CONTEXT.md` and ADRs 0003, 0004, 0005, 0008, and 0009. This audit assesses whether each choice is established (**E**), requirement-driven (**R**), or a project judgment/hyperparameter (**J**). A choice may contain more than one category.

## Executive finding

The analytical spine is defensible: market-model abnormal returns, replay of observed joint factor moves, revaluation/sensitivity pricing, empirical ranks, and historical plus hypothetical stress scenarios are established methods. The project-specific shell is not a scientific fact: exactly eight classes, ten severity levels, the exact matching/backoff order, a three-day primary horizon, basket construction details, and all automatic-action cutoffs require pre-registration and out-of-time testing.

The prototype should therefore claim **a transparent, historically calibrated risk-ranking and stress system**, not a causal forecast or a statistically optimal alert policy. “Empirically backed” applies only where historical evidence was actually used; hypothetical scenarios must remain visibly hypothetical.

## Decision-by-decision audit

| Approved choice | Classification and basis | What must be frozen or tested before the final temporal holdout |
|---|---|---|
| **Classic market-model AR/CAR event study** | **E** for abnormal return and cumulative abnormal return estimation. Event studies and the market model are standard in the primary methodology literature ([MacKinlay 1997](https://www.bu.edu/econ/files/2011/01/MacKinlay-1996-Event-Studies-in-Economics-and-Finance.pdf)); standard daily-return procedures are generally well specified, with known caveats such as event-induced variance and dependence ([Brown & Warner 1985](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X(85)90042-X)). **J** for benchmark, estimation window, event clock, exclusions, test statistic, and overlap treatment. | Pre-register timestamp/time-zone and before/after-close rules; estimation window and gap; benchmark; confound/overlap exclusions; clustering unit; and missing-data handling. Compare constant-mean, market-adjusted, market-model, and—where factor data support it—multi-factor expected returns. Report `[0]`, `[0,+1]`, `[-1,+1]`, and `[-2,+2]`, parametric and sign/rank inference, and event-cluster bootstrap intervals. Choose the primary specification on inner chronological validation, not the largest observed effect. Interpret results as association unless identification supports a causal claim. |
| **Matched historical joint factor scenarios** | **E** for replaying contemporaneous observed shocks and preserving their dependence. Official stress guidance requires internally consistent variables and consideration of historical and hypothetical events ([BCBS stress-testing principles](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d450.pdf)); Strata demonstrates applying historical curve scenarios to the current market and repricing ([official example](https://github.com/OpenGamma/Strata/blob/main/examples/src/main/java/com/opengamma/strata/examples/finance/HistoricalScenarioExample.java)). **R/J** for the factor universe, matching fields, distance, nearest-*k*, scaling, and aggregation. | Freeze eligible history, no-future rule, event deduplication, factor definitions/units, match variables, distance metric, tie-breaks, nearest-*k*, weights, and scaling. Compare raw versus volatility-scaled moves; alternative class/region/sector matching; nearest-*k* grid; and leave-crisis-out results. Score out-of-time scenario distribution by CRPS or proper log score, interval coverage/width, factor-direction accuracy, and stressed-P&L error. Verify that scenario factors are replayed jointly—never independently sampled while claiming historical dependence. |
| **Bump-and-revalue / sensitivity pricing** | **E**. Open-source risk engines implement finite-difference delta, gamma, cross-gamma, scenario application, and revaluation ([ORE user guide](https://www.opensourcerisk.org/wp-content/uploads/2019/05/userguide.pdf); [OpenGamma Strata](https://github.com/OpenGamma/Strata)). **J** for bump convention/size and which instruments may use linear, delta-gamma, duration/convexity, or full repricing. | Freeze absolute/relative/log bump domains, size, central versus one-sided differences, curve rebuild, units, and signs. On a representative instrument set, test half/base/double-bump convergence and compare linear and delta-gamma approximations with full repricing over the candidate shock grid. Publish error by asset/instrument and unsupported-value share; select an approximation domain from held-out P&L error and monotonicity, using full repricing for material nonlinear exposures or large shocks. |
| **Fixed equal-risk cross-asset Reference Basket** | **E** for the equal-risk-contribution construction ([Maillard, Roncalli & Teïletche](https://www.thierry-roncalli.com/download/erc.pdf)). **R** for needing a portfolio-independent, versioned severity numeraire. **J** for constituents/proxies, covariance estimator/lookback, constraints, and freeze/rebalance schedule. A frozen basket is equal-risk only at its calibration date; risk contributions subsequently drift. | Pre-register factor proxies, return frequency, covariance estimator and lookback, volatility floor, constraints, calibration date, versioning, and drift/recalibration rule. Compare equal-risk contribution with equal-notional and inverse-volatility baskets; sample versus shrinkage covariance; 3/6/12/24-month lookbacks; and leave-one-asset-out results. Evaluate out-of-time risk-contribution dispersion, turnover, dominance by any factor, and stability of event-severity ranks. Select the simplest candidate within one standard error of the best validation result. Do not call the basket empirically optimal; call it a frozen comparability convention. |
| **Severity = empirical loss decile 1–10** | **E** for mapping loss through an empirical CDF/quantile estimator; sample quantiles are nonparametric but estimator definitions can differ, especially with finite samples ([Hyndman & Fan 1996](https://robjhyndman.com/papers/sample_quantiles.pdf); [NumPy quantile documentation](https://numpy.org/doc/stable/reference/generated/numpy.quantile.html)). **R** for the human-readable 1–10 contract. **J** for ten equal-frequency bins, reference population, loss statistic, weights, quantile estimator, ties, and refit cadence. The result is ordinal, not an equal-interval economic scale. | Freeze whether loss is mean/median basket P&L across analogues, the event horizon/sign, training-only reference population, weights, quantile algorithm, ties, minimum support, and cutpoint version. Compare 5/10/20 bins, quantile definitions 7/8, weighted/unweighted references, and AR/CAR/stressed-P&L loss definitions. Bootstrap cutpoint intervals and test future-period monotonicity and rank stability. **Impact ≥8 means the top 30% of the frozen reference distribution**, not automatically a rare catastrophe. |
| **Hierarchical backoff: class+region+sector → class+region → class → global** | **E** for the general sparse-cell backoff principle ([Katz 1987](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~danroth/Teaching/CS598-05/Papers/Katz87.pdf)). **R/J** for this exact hierarchy, hard transitions, and minimum support. Region before sector is not universally privileged. | Freeze keys, hierarchy, missing-key rules, effective sample definition, and support/uncertainty trigger. Compare hard backoff with hierarchical/empirical-Bayes shrinkage and similarity-weighted nearest analogues; candidate support levels (for example 20/50/100); and region/sector order permutations. Use leave-cell-out and rolling time-block validation with CRPS/log score, MAE, rank correlation, and tail-interval coverage. Choose the simplest rule within one standard error and always expose the level and effective sample count actually used. |
| **Three-day primary horizon plus one-day display** | **E** for estimating CAR over a declared short event window; **J** for three days as the primary estimand; **R** for a one-day secondary display. Event-study methodology does not make three days universally correct ([MacKinlay 1997](https://www.bu.edu/econ/files/2011/01/MacKinlay-1996-Event-Studies-in-Economics-and-Finance.pdf)). | Pre-register exact index conventions—e.g. whether “three-day” is `[0,+2]` or `[-1,+1]`—plus market close, weekend, holiday, and source-latency mapping. Compare `[0]`, `[0,+1]`, `[-1,+1]`, and `[-2,+2]` on rolling validation for predictive ranking, confounding, and coverage. Keep the one-day view explicitly secondary. Do not select the maximum horizon per event; that is post-selection unless multiplicity is handled. |
| **Hypothetical fallback when evidence is thin** | **E** as stress-testing practice: BCBS explicitly requires historical and hypothetical events and notes nonhistorical scenarios may be necessary when history lacks a severe episode ([BCBS](https://www.bis.org/bcbs/publ/d450.pdf)). **R/J** for what “thin” means, template design, conservatism, and blending. | Pre-register thinness using effective sample size, interval width, or validation coverage; document anchors, plausibility constraints, correlation logic, and governance. Compare historical-only, shrinkage/blended, hypothetical-only, and abstain policies on development periods; vary hypothetical magnitude and correlation assumptions. A hypothetical basket loss may be located on the frozen empirical decile scale, but its provenance must remain `hypothetical` and its score must not be described as directly historically estimated. |
| **Auto-trigger: impact ≥8, confidence ≥0.65, materiality >0** | **J** throughout. Probability estimation and the decision threshold are separate problems; cutoffs should reflect use-case costs and be tuned on data disjoint from model fitting ([scikit-learn threshold guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/classification_threshold.html); [calibration guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/calibration.html)). | First define confidence as a calibratable event, such as the probability that entity/event interpretation is correct; otherwise 0.65 has no probability meaning. Pre-register false-positive/false-negative costs, alert capacity, and primary utility. On chronological development data, evaluate the full grid around impact 7/8/9, confidence .55/.65/.75, and multiple economically meaningful materiality floors. Report calibration, precision/recall, alerts/day, realized loss captured, and net benefit; lock once, then evaluate once on the holdout. `Materiality > 0` admits floating-point dust: add a currency, exposure, or %NAV floor plus numerical tolerance. Without credible costs or alert-budget labels, name this a demo policy, not an optimal policy. |
| **Eight-class taxonomy** | **R**, not statistically canonical. Financial event resources use different granularities: SENTiVENT defines 18 main types and 42 subtypes ([official dataset card](https://huggingface.co/datasets/GillesJacobs/sentivent)); GDELT uses a much broader CAMEO code hierarchy ([official codebook](https://data.gdeltproject.org/documentation/GDELT-Event_Codebook-V2.0.pdf)). Eight stable top-level classes are a product/domain contract. **J** for definitions, multi-label policy, and merge/split boundaries. | Freeze label definitions, positive/negative examples, multi-label/primary-label rule, `Other/Uncertain`, and blind adjudication. Double-annotate a stratified set; report chance-corrected agreement with bootstrap intervals, per-class support/confusion/calibration, and temporal/source out-of-distribution macro-F1. Compare predeclared 6/8/10-class merge/split variants and test whether within-class shock vectors are sufficiently homogeneous. Choose the smallest taxonomy preserving decision-relevant distinctions and tail support. Treat `Other/Uncertain` as abstention/provenance—not a homogeneous shock family. |
| **Separate confidence, materiality, and action priority** | **E** conceptually: statistical uncertainty, economic consequence/exposure, and the action rule are different objects ([NIST SP 800-30 Rev. 1](https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-30r1); [scikit-learn threshold guidance](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/classification_threshold.html)). **R** for the separate user-facing fields. **J** for definitions, normalization, and the priority formula. | Freeze semantics and units: confidence = calibrated reliability of interpretation; materiality = current-portfolio exposure or stressed loss; priority = decision utility/attention order. Compare additive, multiplicative, and cost-based policies at the same analyst alert budget; ablate each input; test monotonicity and rank stability out of time. Avoid double-counting severity inside materiality, and keep source reliability distinct from model confidence. Never present priority as impact or confidence. |

## One pre-registered validation protocol

The choices above should be selected under one locked protocol rather than tuned independently on the final demo cases:

1. **Freeze the data-generating rules:** dataset snapshot; event deduplication; entity and region mapping; event timestamp/market-day mapping; factor universe; return and P&L units; missing-data policy; and one clustering key for all items referring to the same real-world event.
2. **Use nested chronological evaluation:** expanding-time outer folds with an embargo at least as long as the longest event window; inner rolling folds for all model, horizon, matching, basket, backoff, cutpoint, and action-policy choices; one untouched final period. Ordinary random cross-validation would leak future regimes and duplicate-event information. `TimeSeriesSplit` documents time-ordered splits as the relevant baseline ([official documentation](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.model_selection.TimeSeriesSplit.html)).
3. **Lock candidates and objectives in advance:** classification macro-F1; confidence Brier/log loss plus reliability plots; severity rank correlation and ordinal error; scenario CRPS or interval coverage/width; factor-direction accuracy; pricing P&L error; and action utility/precision/recall at a fixed alert budget.
4. **Control selection bias:** report the complete candidate grid and sensitivity curves, use an event-cluster bootstrap for uncertainty, and prefer the simpler candidate within one standard error. Hyperparameter selection itself can overfit unless separated from performance assessment ([Cawley & Talbot 2010](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v11/cawley10a.html)).
5. **Lock once, test once:** after examining the final chronological holdout, do not revise thresholds, bins, horizons, or hierarchy and still report that period as out of sample. Changes require a new version and a new future holdout.

## Required wording corrections

- **Impact 8–10 is “upper three deciles,” not automatically “extreme.”** It covers 30% of the frozen reference distribution by construction.
- **A fixed equal-risk basket is versioned, not permanently equal-risk.** State its calibration date and current contribution drift.
- **Confidence 0.65 is meaningful only for a declared, calibrated probability target.** A composite quality score must not be interpreted as probability.
- **Positive materiality is not materiality.** Use an economic/noise floor, while retaining exact P&L for display.
- **Hypothetical is not empirical.** Preserve scenario provenance in the score, rationale, and audit record.
- **AR/CAR is not automatically causal.** Unless confounding and identification are addressed, describe it as the observed abnormal market reaction associated with an event.

## Bottom line

Proceed with the approved architecture. Its scientific credibility comes from frozen definitions, transparent provenance, nested chronological validation, and sensitivity disclosure—not from presenting project conventions as universal truths. The automatic trigger should remain explicitly provisional until the team can estimate decision costs or at least fix an analyst alert budget and evaluate the threshold frontier out of time.
